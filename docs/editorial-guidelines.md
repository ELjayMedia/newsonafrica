# News On Africa — Editorial Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective:** 2026-09-03
**Owner:** Founding Editorial Team

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the editorial standards for News On Africa (NOA). It applies to all contributors, editors, freelancers, and staff producing content under the NOA brand across every edition and platform.

---

## 2. Editorial values

1. **Authenticity** — Represent African voices and perspectives fairly. Avoid parachute journalism; prioritize local sourcing and context.
2. **Accuracy** — Verify every factual claim with at least two independent sources before publication. Corrections are published promptly when errors are identified.
3. **Independence** — Maintain editorial independence from advertisers, sponsors, and political interests. Clearly label sponsored or native content.
4. **Solutions journalism** — Where appropriate, highlight responses to problems, not just the problems themselves.
5. **Pan-African perspective** — Connect local stories to continental and global contexts without losing local nuance.

---

## 3. Writing standards

### Tone and voice
- Clear, direct, and professional.
- Avoid jargon, sensationalism, and editorializing in news reporting.
- Opinion and analysis pieces must be clearly labeled as such.
- Use active voice and concrete language.

### Story structure
- Lead with the most newsworthy element.
- Provide context early — readers should understand why the story matters by the second paragraph.
- Attribute all claims to named sources where possible. Unattributed claims must be approved by an editor.
- Include relevant data, documents, or evidence to support reporting.

### Headlines
- Accurately reflect the content of the article.
- Optimized for search (SEO) without sacrificing clarity or accuracy.
- No clickbait or misleading phrasing.

---

## 4. Sourcing requirements

- **Named sources preferred.** Anonymous sources require editor approval and must be used only when the information is important and cannot be obtained on the record.
- **Cross-verification.** Key facts must be confirmed by at least two independent sources.
- **Primary sources.** Use original documents, official statements, and direct observation over secondary reports.
- **Diversity of perspective.** Seek out multiple viewpoints, especially those directly affected by the story.

---

## 5. Fact-checking and verification

- All articles undergo editorial review before publication.
- Statistics, data, and direct quotes must be verified against original sources.
- Images and video must be verified for authenticity and proper attribution.
- AI-generated or AI-assisted content must be clearly disclosed and fact-checked by a human editor.

---

## 6. Corrections and retractions

- Errors are corrected promptly with a dated correction note at the top or bottom of the article.
- Significant errors that change the substance of a story warrant a formal correction or retraction.
- Corrections are tracked in the article's correction log (via WordPress custom field).
- The correction policy is publicly accessible on the website.

---

## 7. Sponsored and native content

- All sponsored content is clearly labeled as "Sponsored" or "Paid Partnership."
- Sponsored content is produced separately from editorial content and does not influence editorial decisions.
- Native advertising is disclosed and does not mimic editorial formatting.
- A clear separation is maintained between commercial and editorial teams.

---

## 8. Image and media usage

- All images must be properly licensed or owned by NOA.
- Photojournalism must not misrepresent the scene (no staging or manipulation beyond standard editing).
- AI-generated images must be labeled as such.
- Credit is given to photographers, videographers, and original sources.

---

## 9. Prohibited content

- Hate speech, incitement to violence, or content that targets individuals based on protected characteristics.
- Plagiarism in any form.
- Content that violates the privacy of private individuals without public interest justification.
- Unverified rumors or speculation presented as fact.
- Content that could endanger the safety of sources or staff.

---

## 10. SEO and discoverability

- Articles include relevant keywords naturally integrated into headlines and body text.
- Meta descriptions are written for each article.
- URLs are clean, descriptive, and follow a consistent structure.
- Internal linking is used to connect related coverage.

---

## 11. Review process

1. **Draft** — Contributor submits draft via WordPress.
2. **Editorial review** — Assigned editor reviews for accuracy, tone, structure, and compliance with these guidelines.
3. **Fact-check** — Key claims verified against sources.
4. **Final approval** — Editor-in-chief or designated approver signs off.
5. **Publication** — Scheduled or published immediately upon approval.
6. **Post-publication** — Corrections logged if needed.
